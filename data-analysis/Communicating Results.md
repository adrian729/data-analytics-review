# Communicating Results

## Know Your Audience

Before communicating results, ask:

1. Who is the audience?
2. What do they already know?
3. What do they need to know?
4. What is the clearest way to communicate it?

## Presenting Findings Accurately

- Compare data that uses compatible definitions, units, and periods.
- Use clear labels and appropriate chart scales. Zooming can reveal detail, but should not exaggerate differences or hide context.
- Explain missing data, data-quality issues, and other limitations that affect the conclusions.
- Consider sample size and use appropriate statistical tests to assess uncertainty. Interpret statistical evidence alongside the size and practical importance of the effect.

## Data Visualization

**Data visualization (visualization):** Representing data graphically through charts, maps, and other visuals.

### Visualization Tools

Use these tools to create charts, reports, and dashboards that help people explore data and understand the results of an analysis.

Examples: **Tableau** and **Looker**.

## Dashboards

A **dashboard** brings key information together in a visual display for monitoring and exploration. It can update automatically as new data arrives, depending on how its sources and refresh schedule are configured.

**Strengths**

- Can provide dynamic, interactive views of current data.
- Gives stakeholders convenient access to relevant information.
- Reduces repeated manual reporting when updates are automated.

**Limitations**

- Requires effort to design and set up.
- Can be confusing if poorly organized.
- May display inaccurate results if incoming data is not properly checked or cleaned.
- Still needs maintenance when data sources, structures, or requirements change.

## Reports

A **report** presents information to stakeholders, often as a static snapshot covering a particular period. Reports may be produced periodically.

**Strengths**

- Provides a consistent view of historical results.
- Allows data to be checked, cleaned, and organized before distribution.
- Can be straightforward to create for a defined audience and purpose.

**Limitations**

- Needs to be regenerated or updated for each reporting period.
- A static report does not reflect changes after it is produced.
- Usually offers less interactivity than a dashboard.

Design reports and dashboards around the audience's questions, with a suitable level of detail and interactivity.

[[Spreadsheets#Pivot Tables and Charts|Pivot tables and pivot charts]] can help summarize and visualize spreadsheet data.

## Effective Meetings

An **agenda** sets out the meeting's topics, goals, and planned timing.

### Before

- Define the meeting's purpose, goals, and decisions or questions to address.
- Prepare a clear agenda and share relevant data, notes, or presentations.
- Anticipate questions and prepare the information needed to answer them.

### During

- Keep the discussion focused on the meeting's goals.
- Ask for clarification when something is unclear.
- Record important decisions and agree on next steps.

### After

- Share a concise recap of decisions and actions.
- Confirm who is responsible for each action and when it is due.
