# Project Planning

## Problem Domain

The **problem domain** is the area being analyzed, including the activities that affect or are affected by the problem.

## Stakeholders

**Stakeholders** are people who invest time or resources in a project, have an interest in its outcome, or are affected by its results.

### Executive Team

- **General:** Sets goals, develops strategy, and oversees execution.
- **Needs from analysis:** High-level findings and business implications to understand the big picture; usually needs less technical detail.
- **Example roles:** CEO (overall direction), COO (operations), VP (leads a business area).

### Customer-Facing Teams

- **General:** Works with customers, gathers feedback, and sets expectations.
- **Needs from analysis:** Clear, practical insights about customers to guide daily work and answer specific questions. Stay objective when evaluating their requests.
- **Example roles:** Sales representative (potential customers), support representative (customer problems), account manager (ongoing customer relationships).

### Data Science Team

- **General:** Collaborates on different parts of the data work and combines findings.
- **Needs from analysis:** Detailed data, methods, and findings that colleagues can check, reuse, and build on.
- **Example roles:** Data analyst (answers questions), data scientist (develops models), data engineer (builds data pipelines).

### Working Effectively Together

- Discuss the goals behind each request.
- Agree on boundaries, expectations, responsibilities, and deadlines.
- Anticipate possible obstacles and plan how to handle them.
- Know the project's purpose, scope, and current status.
- Use words and visuals together, and check that everyone shares the same understanding.
- Communicate progress, setbacks, and changes regularly.
- Use [[Analytical Thinking#Reframing|reframing]] when a request needs clarification or a more achievable direction.

### Questions to Resolve Early

1. Who are the primary and secondary stakeholders?
2. Who is responsible for managing the data?
3. Who can provide help, clarification, or approval?

## Scope of Work (SOW)

A **scope of work** is an agreed-upon outline of the work to be performed during a project. It records the goals, requirements, and boundaries so that the team and stakeholders share the same expectations.

### What to Record

Identify the project, analyst or team, and client or sponsor. For a small analysis project, use the following as a practical outline:

| Component | What to specify |
|---|---|
| **Purpose and objectives** | Why the project matters, the question or decision it supports, and what success looks like. |
| **Scope and activities** | The major tasks and analysis to perform, with brief descriptions. Define the data sources, populations, periods, and locations covered. |
| **Exclusions** | Work outside the agreed boundaries, such as implementing recommendations or collecting additional data. |
| **Deliverables** | Specific outputs, including their contents, format, and level of detail: for example, a cleaned dataset, visualization, or report. |
| **Acceptance criteria** | Checkable conditions each deliverable must meet, plus who reviews and accepts it. |
| **Milestones** | Key progress checkpoints and the criteria for considering each one complete. |
| **Timeline** | Expected durations, milestone and delivery dates, and overall completion date. Allow time for review and revision. |
| **Progress reports** | How often updates will be shared, with whom, in what format, and covering which information. |
| **Responsibilities** | Who performs the work, supplies data or expertise, answers questions, and approves decisions. |

**Activity, deliverable, and milestone** describe different aspects of the work. For a traffic study: analyzing sensor data is an activity; a map of the ten most congested locations is a deliverable; approval of the hotspot list is a milestone.

### Assumptions, Dependencies, and Constraints

- **Assumptions:** Conditions used for planning that still need confirmation, such as sufficient sensor coverage.
- **Dependencies:** Inputs or actions the work relies on, such as receiving data access or a stakeholder review.
- **Constraints:** Limits the project must work within, such as time, budget, available tools, or privacy requirements.

Make completion estimates conditional on these needs. If additional collection or specialist input may be required, agree on who provides it and how it affects the schedule.

### Agreeing and Managing the Scope

Develop the scope with stakeholders during the **Ask** phase. Be specific and concise, use quantities where useful, and match commitments to the available data and methods. Causal explanations and estimates of costs or benefits need suitable evidence and expertise.

Agree on the scope before starting. For proposed changes, assess effects on work, resources, and dates; obtain agreement from the responsible decision-maker and record the revised scope. **Scope creep** is uncontrolled expansion of the work without corresponding agreement or adjustments.

The format and level of detail can vary by project or organization.

## Statement of Work

A **statement of work** describes the products or services a vendor or contractor will provide to an organization. It can include objectives, guidelines, deliverables, schedules, and costs.

Both **scope of work** and **statement of work** may be abbreviated **SOW**. Write out the full term when the distinction matters.

Project planning begins during the **Ask** phase of the [[Data Workflows#Data Analysis Process|data analysis process]]. Use [[Analytical Thinking#Asking Effective Questions: SMART|SMART questions]] to clarify requirements.
