A completed case study only earns career value when it is published where others can find, read, and reuse it, so the final move of the capstone is to turn the private analysis into an **[online portfolio](/glossary#online-portfolio)** and decide how that portfolio will live in public.

## Choosing a host that fits your goals

The videos frame host selection with two questions: which platforms align with your interests and passions, and where do you want to spend more time after the program. Kaggle offers a broad data-science community, competitions, and learning threads suited to community-seeking analysts; GitHub suits code-forward sharing of R or Python and documentation history; blog platforms such as Medium, WordPress, and Google Sites favor ownable voice and thought leadership but require extra steps for code; Tableau Public favors interactive visualization. Many analysts eventually use more than one in combination, linking a Tableau viz from a personal site or mirroring a notebook from Kaggle to GitHub, rather than treating the choice as permanent.

## Publishing the portfolio and adding it to Kaggle

Once the host is chosen, create an account and publish the case study as that host expects — a repository with documentation on GitHub, a public notebook or dataset on Kaggle, a site on Google Sites or WordPress, or a draft on Medium — following the checklist of keeping the result personal, simple, relevant, presentable, and unique with cited sources. The program explicitly includes adding the portfolio to Kaggle as a guided activity: publish a dataset or notebook to practice public hosting while the same work can also live elsewhere. Tableau visualizations are public by default on Tableau Public, so linking rather than embedding is often the pragmatic path when the primary site is elsewhere.

## Sharing with others for feedback and reach

The unlimited-potential message after publishing is that the same outline can be reused: complete a second track or scenario, and each addition strengthens the portfolio iteratively. Sharing an [online portfolio](/glossary#online-portfolio) link in the course discussion board is offered as optional and comfort-dependent, but attribution remains expected for any data or creative work cited. The resulting [report](/glossary#report) does not stay a private file — it becomes a [stakeholders](/glossary#stakeholders)-facing presence that recruiters can reach from a resume link, where the clarity of process, visuals, and recommendations does the pre-interview selling.
