A best-in-class submission answers the question as asked, narrates the steps and assumptions, and pairs organized findings with documented analysis — and a best-in-class portfolio feels personal, unique, simple, relevant, and presentable without clutter that distracts from that story.

## Case study moves that stand out

| Move | What to do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Answer the question | Lead with a one-slide overview of your finding on the resale-performance prompt from Data Partners Real Estate — e.g., flagging the underperforming price band — before detailing method. | Keeps the narrative tied to the business task and metrics, not the tool. |
| Show your process | Use slide titles as a story, define metrics explicitly, and attach speaker notes with key assumptions and SQL/spreadsheet documentation. | Recruiters screen for thought process and problem-solving, not perfection. |
| Act on the data | Close with specific recommendations and evidence, explaining background the audience may not know. | Transforms analysis into decisions the stakeholder can approve. |

## Portfolio standard — personal, unique, simple

| Principle | Example from the readings |
|---|---|
| Personal | Bill Yost's cancer-journey Tableau viz with handwritten notes ("Mom if reading send more cookies") shows health data and voice simultaneously. |
| Unique | Jesse Mostipak and Meg Risdal's Kaggle notebooks mix palmer-penguins practice with passion projects like video-game analysis. |
| Simple | A GitHub R-tips master index with a table of contents and light cover art keeps navigation clean while still showing personality. |
| Relevant & presentable | Tailor highlighted skills to the job description, keep the site current, and link only work you can permission — employer-owned work needs explicit approval and open datasets need citation to avoid plagiarism. |

## Checklists

A **[portfolio checklist](/glossary#portfolio-checklist)** covers the pages employers expect: concise biography, contact page with email or LinkedIn, resume, career accomplishments such as certifications or events, and an optional photo. A **[case study checklist](/glossary#case-study-checklist)** covers the narrative arc: introduction that states purpose and assumptions, problems where you identify and fact-check the major issues, solutions with alternatives and pros and cons, conclusion summarizing takeaways and learning, and next steps that recommend who should do what and when.
