This is the capstone: the certificate's Ask-Prepare-Process-Analyze-Share-Act loop run end to end on a real business scenario and packaged so an employer can read it. It opens by defining the three things a graduate leaves with — the capstone project, a case study written up like the time-boxed exercise employers hand out, and a public portfolio of case studies — and by laying out the checklists that separate best-in-class work from a tutorial dataset.

Then the two tracks diverge. Track A hands you the business task and the data (Cyclistic bike-share, Bellabeat wearables) so you can prove you can execute a given brief; Track B asks you to pick a public dataset on a genuine interest and drive the whole process yourself. Either way the finish line is the same: publishing the case study as an online portfolio, defending it in an interview through a pitch and problem walkthrough, negotiating the offer, and finally turning the certificate into something that compounds — with a closing unit on using generative AI as one tool in the analyst's workflow.
