Four words get used interchangeably in casual conversation and mean different things here. Getting them apart is the whole point of this lesson.

## The vocabulary

[Data](/glossary#data) is a collection of facts — numbers, pictures, videos, words, measurements, observations. A [dataset](/glossary#dataset) is a collection of data that can be manipulated or analyzed as one unit.

[Data analysis](/glossary#data-analysis) is the collection, transformation, and organization of data in order to draw conclusions, make predictions, and drive informed decision-making. A [data analyst](/glossary#data-analyst) is the person who does it.

[Data analytics](/glossary#data-analytics) is the broader science of data — the umbrella covering the data itself, the analysis, the tools, and the [data ecosystem](/glossary#data-ecosystem) around them.

The distinction that actually gets tested is **analyst versus scientist**. [Data science](/glossary#data-science) creates *new questions* from raw data, modeling the unknown. Data analysis finds *answers to existing questions* using data that is already available. One invents the question, the other answers it.

## The three disciplines

Data science as a field splits into three, and the thing that separates them is *how many decisions you know you want to make before you begin*:

| Discipline | Decisions | Excellence |
|---|---|---|
| Statistics | A few important ones, under uncertainty | **Rigor** — protecting decision-makers from wrong conclusions |
| Machine learning / AI | Many, automated, under uncertainty | **Performance** — high accuracy at scale |
| Analytics | You don't know yet — you're seeking unknown unknowns | **Speed** — how fast you can explore a lot of data and surface what's worth escalating |

That framing is worth keeping, because it explains why analytics work feels different from the other two. You are not being graded on the rigor of a single conclusion or the accuracy of a model. You are being graded on how quickly you can search a large space and find the thing worth bringing to someone.

The universe of data is too large for one person to know all of it, so specialization is unavoidable — and which discipline fits is as much personality as skill. Rigor-driven people gravitate to statistics, performance-driven people to ML, and people who thrive on open-ended exploration and ambiguity land in analytics.

## The data ecosystem

The data ecosystem is the collection of elements that interact to produce, manage, store, organize, analyze, and share data: hardware, software, people, and [the cloud](/glossary#cloud) — data stored and accessed online rather than on a local drive.

Ecosystems are domain-specific, and the examples are worth remembering because they show the same structure repeating:

- **Retail** — purchase history feeds demand prediction.
- **Human resources** — labor market data feeds recruiting and retention.
- **Agriculture** — weather and geological data feed crop yield prediction.
- **Environmental science** — coral reef monitoring data feeds conservation decisions.
