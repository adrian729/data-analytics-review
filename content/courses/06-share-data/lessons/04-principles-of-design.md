Even the most accurate numbers cannot communicate until they are given form, which is why the module treats visualizing as an art practice analysts can borrow from deliberately. The sources organize that borrowing into three layers: the material elements you manipulate, the principles that guide how you manipulate them, and the overall test of whether the result communicates — and they ground all three in concrete charts you can save and re-study rather than abstract rules.

## Elements of art you build with

The video isolates five elements that add most to [data visualization](/glossary#data-visualization): line, shape, color, space, and movement — not the only art elements, but those that most improve effectiveness. Lines may be curved or straight, thick or thin, vertical, horizontal, or diagonal and provide structure and [visual form](/glossary#visual-form); shapes should stay two-dimensional so three-dimensional objects do not complicate the view, and symmetry or a recognizable silhouette (a circle as a pie chart, countries on a map) reduces viewer effort. Color is hue (name), intensity (brightness or dullness), and value (lightness or darkness — dark values are shades, light values are tints — and shifting value draws attention). Space is the area between, around, and in objects and should be balanced so bar gaps are smaller than bar widths; movement creates flow or action as literal animation or the path the eye is led through.

## Nine principles for arranging those elements

The reading states nine principles to apply while building and to check after finishing. **[Balance](/glossary#balance)** is creating aesthetic appeal and clarity by evenly distributing visual elements; the motivation-and-energy column chart is balanced not by symmetry but by colors, widths, and spacing that keep one side from distracting. **[Emphasis](/glossary#emphasis)** is arranging elements to focus attention on important information; the "Where Slang Comes From" heat map uses color and value intensity to emphasize high-search states so the trend reads without exact values. **[Movement](/glossary#movement)** is guiding the eyes from one part to another, or literal animation; the combo chart's average line pulls the eye left to right, while the Wealth and Health of Nations animation traces centuries but warns that interactivity hands story control to the driver. **[Pattern](/glossary#pattern)** is using similar elements to demonstrate trends and relationships; the stacked column chart of book sales keeps a consistent color per genre so the rise of fantasy and sci-fi remains comparable. **[Repetition](/glossary#repetition)** is repeating elements to demonstrate meaning — the same chart's repeated colors signal distinct data sets. **[Proportion](/glossary#proportion)** is using relative size and arrangement to demonstrate information; a larger dashboard chart draws attention, and the donut-eaten pie chart uses slice size and color to make counts focal while preserving true ratios. The last three are post-build checks: **[Rhythm](/glossary#rhythm)** is creating movement and flow to engage an audience, **[Variety](/glossary#variety)** is using different kinds of elements to engage without confusing, and **[Unity](/glossary#unity)** is using elements that complement each other for overall clarity — a disjointed visual fails unity no matter how well earlier principles were applied.

## Four elements that make a visualization succeed

David McCandless's Venn is the final quality check: information (data itself), story or concept (narrative that makes data meaningful; without it the work is informative but not inspiring), goal or function (what makes data useful and usable), and visual form or metaphor (structure and beauty that makes data readable). Two saved examples balance all four. Best in Show plots popularity against an objective data score on a four-square grid with dog-shaped marks whose size and facing encode detail, dividing breeds into Overrated, Rightly Ignored, Hot Dogs, and Overlooked Treasures. When Sea Levels Attack projects 8,000 years of rise with city silhouettes and inset continent maps that make inundation immediate. Both pair a dataset with a story and a goal through a [visual form](/glossary#visual-form) that is structural and metaphorical — the same refined execution, deep attention to detail with art elements, that the module names alongside clear meaning and sophisticated use of contrast as the three essentials of an effective visual.
