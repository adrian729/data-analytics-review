A finished LinkedIn profile does two jobs: it helps recruiters find you, and it helps the people you connect with actually understand who they're connecting with. A clear, high-resolution, recognizable photo and a fully filled-out profile (not just a name and headline) both pull real weight here — profiles that look unfinished read as unfinished.

## Where to find other data analysts

**Online:**

- Newsletters (e.g. Data Elixir) for a steady stream of names and topics worth following.
- Hackathons and communities like Kaggle, which come with their own forums.
- Meetups — usually local, usually with a posted schedule you can join virtually.
- LinkedIn and Twitter hashtags (`#dataanalyst`, `#dataanalytics`, `#datascience`) for both finding people and posting your own questions.
- Webinars, often recorded, often free, with a panel worth following afterward.

**In person:**

- Conferences, including ones focused on under-represented groups in the field.
- Professional associations and societies, many free to join.
- User communities and summits built around a specific tool.
- Non-profits promoting the ethical use of data science.

## Why both matter

In-person connections tend to be easier to build quickly — a real conversation moves faster than a message thread. Online connections aren't limited by geography, so they're the ones that put you in touch with people outside your immediate area. Neither replaces the other; building both gives you more of the field to actually learn from, and mentors are one of the more durable things a network produces once it's established.
