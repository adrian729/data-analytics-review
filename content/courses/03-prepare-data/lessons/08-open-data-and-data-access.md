**[Openness](/glossary#openness)**, the sixth aspect of data ethics, is the promotion of free access, usage, and sharing of data. It doesn't override the other five aspects — data can be open and still demand transparency, privacy, and consent. It just means that, on top of those, the data meets three further conditions:

1. **Available and accessible** to the public as a complete dataset, ideally downloadable in a convenient, modifiable form.
2. **Reusable and redistributable** — provided under terms that permit reuse, including combining it with other datasets.
3. **Open to universal participation** — usable by anyone, with no restriction limiting it to a specific field, person, or group.

Data only counts as [open data](/glossary#open-data) once it clears all three. Sites like data.gov meet the bar; a dataset licensed for use by one industry only does not.

## The case for it, and the tension underneath it

Open data lets credible datasets get combined and reused far more widely, with real upside: faster disease detection through combined health data, better accountability when government data is public, and broader access to community services. But third-party data — collected by an entity with no direct relationship to the people it describes — is exactly the kind of data that ends up open, and it can include or enable inference of [personally identifiable information](/glossary#personally-identifiable-information). The open-data debate is really this: how far openness should extend before it starts trading away the privacy of the people the data describes.

## Interoperability

None of this works without **[data interoperability](/glossary#data-interoperability)** — the ability of different data systems and services to connect and share data with each other. It's what lets a doctor's system send a prescription straight to a pharmacy's system: the two databases are built to understand each other. Interoperability is the main practical obstacle to open data delivering on its promise, since it requires real cooperation, not just a public-access policy, between the systems involved.
