Data analytics is about solving problems, not just plugging numbers into a platform. The [data analysis process](/glossary#data-analysis-process) — Ask, Prepare, Process, Analyze, Share, Act — gives that problem-solving a shape. This lesson walks through what each phase means in practice.

## The six phases, applied

**Ask.** Define the problem you're trying to solve. Make sure you fully understand the stakeholder's expectations, focus on the actual problem rather than distractions, and keep an open line of communication.
Self-check: *What are my stakeholders saying their problem is? How can I help them resolve it?*

**Prepare.** Decide what data you need and how to organize it — what metrics to measure, where the data lives, what security it needs.
Self-check: *What do I need to figure out how to solve this problem? What research do I need to do?*

**Process.** Clean the data: find incorrectly entered values, strip extra spaces, remove duplicate entries, check for bias.
Self-check: *What errors or inaccuracies might get in the way? How can I make my data more consistent?*

**Analyze.** Sort and format the data so you can perform calculations, combine sources, and build result tables.
Self-check: *What story is my data telling me? Who needs this product or service?*

**Share.** Summarize results with clear, enticing visuals — graphs, dashboards — so stakeholders can see how you solved their problem.
Self-check: *How can I make this engaging and easy to understand? What would help me if I were the listener?*

**Act.** Use what you learned. Give stakeholders recommendations they can act on.
Self-check: *How can I use share-phase feedback to actually meet the stakeholder's needs?*

## Structured thinking

Breaking the six phases into small, manageable steps is itself a skill: [structured thinking](/glossary#structured-thinking). It has four parts — recognizing the current problem or situation, organizing available information, revealing gaps and opportunities, and identifying your options. When your role and expectations feel like they're pulling you in different directions, structured thinking is what gets you back on track: it fills in the gaps and tells you exactly what you still need.
