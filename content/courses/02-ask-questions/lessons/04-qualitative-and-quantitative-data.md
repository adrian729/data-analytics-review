Data comes in two flavors. [Quantitative data](/glossary#quantitative-data) is a specific, objective measure — a number, quantity, or range — collected with tools like structured interviews, surveys, and polls. [Qualitative data](/glossary#qualitative-data) is a subjective, explanatory measure of a quality or characteristic, collected with tools like focus groups, social media text analysis, and in-person interviews.

The short version: quantitative data generally gives you the **what**; qualitative data generally gives you the **why**.

## Worked example: a movie theater chain

An analyst tracking three trends — attendance over time, concession-stand profitability, and evening audience preferences — already has quantitative data for all three:

- Attendance averaged 550/month, rising to 1,600/month in holiday months. Looking at a full year instead of the last 3 months (which happened to miss a holiday) avoided a misleading conclusion.
- Concession profit margin was under 5% on average purchases of $20 or less.
- The 7:30 PM showtime was the most popular, suggesting the 8:00 PM showtime should be moved to 8:30 PM.

None of that explains *why*. So the analyst adds a qualitative survey asking things like: *What went into your decision to see a movie here today?* and *Which showtime do you prefer, 8:00 PM or 8:30 PM, and why?* Maybe attendance patterns trace back to reclining chairs, a menu item no other theater has, or how far people are driving from nearby restaurants — reasons quantitative data alone never surfaces.

Analysts generally use both types of data together: quantitative surfaces the pattern, qualitative explains it.
