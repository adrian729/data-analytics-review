The interview does not end when you stand up; the notes frame the close as three moves — ask thoughtful questions while you still have the room, handle an offer with measured comparison against your plan, and send a concise, personalized thank-you that Gemini can draft and you must own — before deciding next steps.

## Questions to ask your interviewers

Strong questions show that you listened and that you are already thinking about the work and its [stakeholders](/glossary#stakeholders): what a typical week on this team looks like, how success is measured in the first 90 days, which stakeholders the role serves most, what decision the team's current analysis is trying to enable, and what the interviewer finds most rewarding or most challenging about the team. Prepare three to five, prioritize two, and keep each answerable in a minute so the conversation stays conversational; the notes frame this as preparation, not performance, and as a chance to decide if you want the role as much as they want you.

## Handling offers and next moves

An offer is data to compare, not a reflex to accept on the spot; the notes describe reviewing the written package against the job search plan, the day-to-day work you heard about, and any competing processes you have underway, then responding within the timeline the employer gave. The generally-true habit is to thank them promptly, ask clarifying questions about scope, growth, and compensation components before countering, and keep the job search tracker updated through the decision so follow-ups stay clean; if you decline, do so professionally to preserve the relationship with the team you just met.

## Thank-you note drafted with Gemini

The thank-you note is brief, specific, and human — a sentence of genuine thanks, one concrete callback to something discussed, a one-line reminder of the value you would bring that maps to their need, and a forward-looking close — and the notes suggest drafting it with Gemini rather than starting from a blank page. Prompt shape: "Draft a 4–5-sentence thank-you for [role] at [company], reference [specific discussion point], restate [one relevant accomplishment] as it relates to [team need], professional and warm tone, no clichés." Edit every draft for specificity; an [elevator pitch](/glossary#elevator-pitch) that was generic does not become specific by being pasted into a thank-you, and a good [prompt](/glossary#prompt) still requires you to verify names, titles, and details before sending within 24 hours.

## Make your next move

The notes close the course — and the certificate — with a "make your next move" and congratulations moment that is intentionally left open; the durable takeaway is that the search artifacts now compound — the tailored resume, the portfolio, the STAR stories, and the tracker become the system you reuse for the next opening, with Gemini and related tools as ongoing drafting help rather than as a one-time course exercise.
