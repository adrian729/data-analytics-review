Interviewing is not memorization but rehearsal under constraints — know the format, structure stories so a listener can follow them, craft a concise pitch, organize evidence, and practice aloud, using [generative AI](/glossary#generative-ai) tools for drafts and feedback while keeping every answer truthful and specific to work you can defend.

## Formats and best practices — cheatsheet

| Format | What it tests | How to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter screen (15–30 min) | Fit, logistics, salary range, communication | Tight [elevator pitch](/glossary#elevator-pitch), 2 quantifiable bullets per past role, questions about the team |
| Hiring manager / panel | Role alignment and trade-offs | One case study you can walk end-to-end, metrics you tracked and why |
| Technical / case exercise | How you approach a messy ask under time | Narrate your data analysis process phases, name assumptions, state what you would validate next |
| Behavioral round | Collaboration and judgment | 4–5 [STAR](/glossary#star-method) stories covering conflict, failure, initiative, and explaining complexity |

Across formats the notes emphasize being on time, concise, and evidence-backed, with a follow-up thank you for each conversation.

## Answer with STAR — the [STAR method](/glossary#star-method)

The **[STAR method](/glossary#star-method)** structures a behavioral answer into Situation, Task, Action, Result so a listener can track what was at stake and what you actually did, rather than hearing a general claim. Prompt shape with Gemini: "Turn this bullet [paste] into a 90-second STAR answer, keep Situation/Task to one sentence each, focus Action on my decisions, and end with a measurable Result; flag any vague claim to replace with a number I can verify." Keep the practitioner (Julia) framing as noted — handling challenging questions with confidence — without inventing quoted coaching that the notes do not provide; the durable habit is to rehearse one STAR story until it fits in under two minutes before adding the next.

## Craft the pitch with Gemini, organize with Gemini Notebook

Your interview pitch is the [elevator pitch](/glossary#elevator-pitch) tuned for this employer: who you are, what you do best, and why this team, in 30–45 seconds. Prompt shape: "Given this career identity statement [paste] and this posting [paste], draft a 3-sentence pitch that links one past accomplishment to one team need, no buzzwords." For organization the notes point to Gemini Notebook as the place to centralize the posting, your STAR stories, portfolio links, and questions you want to ask, so you can ask it to surface gaps — a story you have not yet STAR-structured, a portfolio piece you have not linked to a likely question — before you walk in.

## Practice with Gemini Live and recruiter strategy (Sydney)

**Gemini Live** is described in the notes as a conversational practice surface — you speak, it responds — and a recruiter perspective (Sydney) is attached to interview strategy without quoted content. Generally-true rehearsal is to run a timed mock where Gemini Live plays the interviewer, asking a [prompt](/glossary#prompt)-driven mix of behavioral and technical questions, then to transcribe and edit the answer for filler words, missing metrics, and too-long setups, alternating with [Interview Warmup](/glossary#interview-warmup) on grow.google/interview-warmup for its talking-points and job-terms insights. Treat AI feedback as a second opinion to cut and clarify, not as coaching to invent experience, and verify the live syllabus for the exact Gemini Live and Notebook exercises this course requires.
